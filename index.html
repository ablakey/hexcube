A hex grid is a slice of a cube

Give every small cube a 3D coordinate (x, y, z).

Keep only the cubes where x + y + z = 0 and throw the rest away.

That single diagonal slice through the 9×9×9 block is a flat, tilted plane — and its cells line up as a perfect 2D hexagonal grid. The slice's overall outline is itself a regular hexagon, so you get a big hexagon made of small hexagons.

The hexagons aren't drawn — they're just the cubes themselves: a cube projected onto a 2D plane (viewed straight down the (1, 1, 1) diagonal) has a six-sided silhouette. That direction is exactly the plane's normal — not a coincidence — which is why the slice appears face-on and every cube reads as a hexagon.

So hex coordinates aren't special: they're just cube coordinates constrained to sum to zero.
